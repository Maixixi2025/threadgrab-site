Bluesky Moderation Inbox 2026: Strikes & Appeals Guide

November 3, 2026 · 8 min read · ThreadGrab

On October 30, 2026, Bluesky shipped something its power users had been asking for since the platform's November 2025 strike-system announcement: a moderation inbox inside the app. Until now, if Bluesky labeled your post, suspended your account, or acted on a report you filed, you usually found out by email — or, very often, you didn't find out at all. The inbox changes that. Here's what it does, what it deliberately leaves out, and what long-form creators and archivists should do about it.

Why Bluesky moved moderation out of email

Bluesky gave two reasons in its announcement, signed by trust-and-safety lead Aaron Rodericks: coverage and reach.

Coverage was a volume problem. Bluesky could only email users about the most serious decisions — primarily account and content takedowns. Lesser actions, like the adult-content labels that flood any large social network, usually came with no message at all. A label could quietly change how your post was displayed without you ever being told.

Reach was an architecture problem, and it's the one that matters for the AT Protocol's decentralization story. Bluesky accounts can live on independent Personal Data Servers (PDS) that Bluesky doesn't operate. Those accounts can still be read and moderated inside the Bluesky app — but Bluesky had no email address for them. If your PDS was run by a third party, enforcement notices simply never arrived.

The inbox solves reach by living in the app instead of anyone's mail client. "Anyone who uses the app can see their reports, get notices about their account, and file appeals there."

Three things, one inbox

ComponentWhat it showsCreator angle
ReportsA record of every post or account you reported, plus an update once Bluesky reviewed it (action taken or not)File reports with a comment — Bluesky says explaining which guideline was violated makes review more accurate
Actions on your accountModeration decisions about your account and content, each appealable from the same screenOne exception: a full suspension locks you out, so suspended users appeal from the app's login screen instead

The sample interface Bluesky published splits the inbox into two tabs — Your reports and Your account — with a yellow banner above the account history when strikes exist. Selecting the banner opens a panel: "We've taken action on your account for violating our Community Guidelines, and strikes have been added to your record. More violations will lead to stronger enforcement, including suspension."

The strike system: what we know vs. what's still unpublished

Bluesky's November 19, 2025 strike-system post defined four severity levels — critical, high, moderate, and low risk — and committed to two things: richer enforcement notices and this inbox. The inbox arrived a little over eleven months later. Here's the honest state of the public record:

// What Bluesky HAS published about strikes (as of Nov 2026)
severityLevels: ["critical", "high", "moderate", "low"]
critical     -> immediate permanent ban
accumulation -> temporary suspension -> permanent ban
strikesDecay -> true (period unpublished)

// What the inbox banner shows
"a handful of strikes" | "at risk of permanent suspension"

// What it does NOT show
violationCount | severityLevel | distanceToNextThreshold | suspensionEndDate

Compare that to Google's Ads strike system (warning → 3-day hold → 7-day hold → suspension, strikes expiring after 90 days) or TikTok's (24/48-hour restrictions → 72-hour view-only → notice near permanent ban, decay period unstated). Bluesky's model is closer to TikTok's: it names the outcome and confirms decay, but publishes neither the number of strikes behind each escalation step nor the time a strike takes to drop off. Bluesky says more detail is coming "soon."

The catch for existing accounts

Two quirks worth knowing before you rely on the inbox:

It starts empty. Even if your account already carries strikes, the inbox does not load past enforcement history — the entries below the banner begin accumulating from launch day. Your current standing is reflected in the banner from the start, though. No banner means good standing.

Beta-only, app-only. You must opt in via beta features in settings, and notifications arrive by default for three categories (your reports, notices about your account, labels on your posts) — each toggleable. The October 30 post doesn't say whether the inbox will reach third-party AT Protocol clients, or whether labels from independent moderation services running Ozone (Bluesky's open-source moderation tool) will appear in it. As written, it covers Bluesky's own trust-and-safety decisions inside Bluesky's own app.

What this means for long-form creators

If you publish long-form on Bluesky — the platform's answer to X Articles — moderation transparency is now part of your publishing stack. Three practical moves:

1. Check your standing before you publish. One glance at the banner tells you whether your account is clean. If you're mid-way through a serialized long-form project, a surprise suspension with no visible history would be devastating; the banner at least makes cumulative risk legible.

2. Export before you appeal. If the inbox shows strikes or actions against your account, archive your long-form posts before filing an appeal. If an appeal fails and escalates to suspension, your appeal path runs through the login screen — and your content access runs through whatever backup you made. Our Bluesky post archive guide covers the API and export methods.

3. Build a moderation-aware export habit. The most durable workflow treats moderation state as export metadata:

# Pseudocode: moderation-aware Bluesky export
state = fetch_account_standing()          # via app; no public API yet
if state.has_strikes or state.at_risk:
    export_all_posts(format="markdown")   # your full archive, now
    log(state.strike_summary)             # attach to archive for context
else:
    export_new_posts_since_last_run()     # normal incremental backup

Since Bluesky has not (yet) exposed strike data via a public API, the check itself is manual — a monthly "open the app, look at the banner" habit alongside your scheduled export. It's two minutes, and it's the difference between knowing your risk and discovering it as a suspension notice.

FAQ

What is the Bluesky moderation inbox?

The moderation inbox is a beta feature Bluesky launched on October 30, 2026 inside its app. It collects three things in one place: reports you have filed (with review outcomes), enforcement actions taken on your account or posts (with an appeal button), and your account's overall standing under the strike system — including whether you are at risk of permanent suspension.

How do I enable the moderation inbox on Bluesky?

Open the Bluesky app settings and switch on beta features. The inbox appears with two tabs — Your reports and Your account. Note that the inbox starts empty even for accounts that already carry strikes: past enforcement history is not loaded retroactively, though current standing is shown in the banner from day one.

How many strikes trigger a permanent Bluesky ban?

Bluesky has not published the exact threshold. Its November 2025 strike-system post defined four severity levels — critical, high, moderate, and low risk — and stated that critical violations lead to an immediate permanent ban, while accumulated lower-severity strikes escalate from temporary suspensions to permanent bans. The inbox banner shows only whether you have "a handful of strikes" or are at risk of permanent suspension.

Do Bluesky strikes expire?

Yes. According to Bluesky, strikes do not remain on your account permanently and come off your record over time, but the company has not stated the decay period. More detail on the strike system was promised "soon" in the October 30, 2026 announcement.

Does the moderation inbox work for accounts on independent PDS servers?

Yes — and that is the point. Before the inbox, moderation notices went by email, which Bluesky could not send to accounts hosted on independent Personal Data Servers (PDS). Because the inbox lives inside the app rather than in a mail client, any app user can see their reports, account notices, and file appeals regardless of which PDS hosts their account.

Bottom Line

The moderation inbox is the first piece of Bluesky's promised transparency stack that actually shipped — eleven months after the strike system was announced. It's genuinely useful: coverage for the label flood that email never reached, reach for the decentralized accounts email could never find, and a visible strike banner that turns "surprise suspension" into a legible risk. But until Bluesky publishes the strike thresholds, decay periods, and an API, creators are still flying with an incomplete instrument panel. Check your standing, archive your long-form work, and re-read that banner once a month.
